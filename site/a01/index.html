Nine people listened to a 30-minute podcast while electrodes recorded from their brains. Ariel Goldstein, Uri Hasson and colleagues reported this in 2022.

Shared principlesPredict, compare, keep the context

The 2022 study found three principles shared by brains and the model. Both predict the next word before it appears. Both compare that prediction with what actually comes. And both represent each word in its context.

Across many models, Martin Schrimpf, Evelina Fedorenko and colleagues found in 2021 that the models better at predicting the next word were also better at predicting brain responses to sentences. The strongest captured nearly all of the explainable variance. Prediction also runs through human learning, as the power of prediction shows.

LearningVery different diets

The learning is very different. Children become fluent on tens of millions of words, heard in a social and physical world. As Michael Frank pointed out in 2023, large models are trained on around four or five orders of magnitude more.

And prediction isn’t the whole of cognition. A model has no body, no goals of its own, and no memory of you unless one is built in. It predicts what text is likely, not what is true.

A cautionSimilar computations, different minds

Similar computations don’t mean similar minds. These studies compare language processing, mostly listening, in a handful of people and models.

Explain AI as a prediction engine

Tell students that AI predicts likely text, so they expect fluent errors. Use prediction in your own teaching: ask students to guess before they read, because a wrong guess sharpens attention. And remind them that their learning runs on effort and meaning, not volume.
