Researchers asked two versions of ChatGPT to write 84 short literature reviews, then checked all 636 citations against library databases. With the older model, GPT-3.5, 55% of the citations did not exist. With GPT-4, 18% did not. William Walters and Esther Isabelle Wilder reported this in 2023.

The fabricated citations looked real, with plausible titles, often in real journals. Even among real citations, many had substantive errors, such as wrong volumes or pages: 43% for the older model, and 24% for the newer one.

Lateral readingHow fact checkers check

In a study of how people judge websites, Sam Wineburg and Sarah McGrew compared 10 professional fact checkers, 10 PhD historians and 25 Stanford students. Asked to find the more reliable of two websites, every fact checker succeeded, against half of the historians and a fifth of the students.

The fact checkers’ method was lateral reading: leaving the page after a quick look, and opening new tabs to see what other sources say about it. The historians and students read vertically, staying on the page and judging it by how it looked: its logo, its domain name, its About page.

A cautionOlder models, a small study

The citation study used models from 2023, and the lateral reading study was small. But the principle holds: fluent output is not evidence.

Make verification the assignment

Require students to verify every AI-supplied source, and show how they did it. Teach lateral reading explicitly: leave the page, and check who is behind a claim. And set an assignment where AI output contains planted errors to find.
