Pillar 1Synapses

It is true that connections form rapidly after birth. In early childhood, synapse density peaks at levels roughly 50% higher than in adults, and stays high until puberty. But more synapses do not mean more learning, and learning continues as connections are pruned and refined.

Pillar 2Critical periods

They are real for some sensory and motor systems. Vision is the classic example. But there is little evidence of such windows for most of what we learn in school, like reading, history or chemistry.

Pillar 3Enriched environments

The research was largely with rats, comparing barren cages to more normal ones. It does not show that extra stimulation builds super brains.

The grain of truthEarly experience matters

Early experience does matter, and good early education helps many children. But the brain stays plastic. Adults learn new languages, skills and whole fields, as The Learning Brain series showed.

For teaching

Tell students, especially returning and adult learners, that it is not too late. Build on what they already know. And design for steady practice over time, rather than a single window. Share this tip with students: it’s never too late to learn something hard.
