The idea seems to come from a stretch. The psychologist William James wrote that people use only a small part of their mental resources. In 1936, a foreword to a bestselling book turned that into a falsely precise ten percent, credited to James.

Three lines of evidenceImaging, injury, energy

Brain imaging tells a different story. Over the course of a day, activity shows up across the whole brain, even during sleep. There is no silent 90% waiting to be switched on.

Brain injury tells the same story. There is almost no area that can be damaged without some loss of ability. If 90% were spare, most injuries would do nothing.

And the brain is expensive to run. It uses a large share of the body’s energy. Evolution would not keep paying for tissue that sits idle.

The grain of truthEfficiency, not waste

Not every neuron fires at once. At any moment, only some areas are working hard. That is efficiency, not waste.

Why it mattersNo trick to unlock

The myth suggests a hidden reserve, unlocked by a trick. Real gains come from something slower: knowledge and skill built through practice.

For teaching

Replace the promise of hidden potential with a plan for practice. Show students how much they have already built. And be wary of products that promise to unlock the brain. Share this tip with students: there is no hidden 90%; practice is how ability grows.
