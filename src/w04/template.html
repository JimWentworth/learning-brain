Run it in class

  1. Run the activity with the class voting on each word.
  2. Ask students to list three words in this course that also have an everyday meaning.

Ask the room

  • Which everyday meaning got in the way?
  • Which words in this course mean something different from everyday use?
  • How would a student know that a familiar word has a special meaning here?

Connect it to your course

When you use an everyday word in its technical sense, flag it: “In statistics, significant has a precise meaning…” Contrast the two meanings explicitly, and come back to them.

Good to know

Students who learned the subject in another language may know the technical meaning well but not the English term, or the reverse.

Academic writing uses a layer of vocabulary that is rare in everyday English but common across disciplines. Averil Coxhead’s Academic Word List gathers 570 word families that, together, made up about 10% of the words in a 3.5-million-word corpus of academic texts, but only 1.4% of the words in fiction.

On top of that sits each discipline’s own vocabulary, and the trickiest part of it: ordinary words with specialised meanings.

Academic wordsCommon in courses, rare at home

The Academic Word List was built from texts across 28 subject areas in arts, commerce, law and science, and leaves out the 2,000 most frequent English words. Words such as “analyse”, “assume” and “derive” appear across disciplines but seldom in conversation, so students meet them mostly in coursework.

False friendsWords students think they know

A word students have never seen tells them it is new. A familiar word with a technical meaning doesn’t. “Significant” sounds like “important”; “theory” sounds like “guess”; “work” sounds like “effort”. Students may read past these words, confident that they understood, while building the wrong idea.

For learningName the shift

Making the contrast explicit, and returning to it over time, turns a hidden trap into a word students know they need to learn. Topic 2 describes how repeated, varied encounters and recall build word knowledge.
