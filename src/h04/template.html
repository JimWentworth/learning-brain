Exercise and the brain Move your body; help your brain

If you have ever felt clearer after a walk, you are not imagining it. Movement changes the brain, over months and within minutes. For memory, the timing matters.

In a year-long trial with 120 older adults, reported by Kirk Erickson and colleagues in 2011, people who walked briskly three days a week grew their hippocampus, a key memory region, by about 2%. A stretching group lost about 1.4%.

But those adults were in their late fifties to seventies, not students. And on the study’s memory test, both groups improved by similar amounts. So it shows the brain can change, not that a gym habit will raise your grades.

One sessionA small boost, within minutes

What about a single session? A 2012 meta-analysis of 79 studies, by Yu-Kai Chang and colleagues, found a small boost to thinking during and after one bout of exercise. In 2025, the same team pooled 30 meta-analyses, covering more than 18,000 people, and put the average effect at small to medium, with the largest benefits measured after exercise rather than during it.

A cautionModest effects, small studies

Effects are modest and vary from study to study. Many experiments are small and run in labs. Exercise will not replace studying, but it is a cheap way to support it.

Try this

Take a brisk walk of 10 to 20 minutes before a study session. After a hard session, move again instead of scrolling. Don’t try to memorize while working out hard. And build movement into every week, not just exam week.
