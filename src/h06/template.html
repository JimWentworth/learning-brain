Stress and the exam Nerves are energy; point them at the test

Your heart races, your palms sweat, and the first question looks unfamiliar. Exam stress is common, and two simple techniques have been tested with students.

Worry takes up working memory, the mental space you need to solve problems. So at the University of Chicago, Gerardo Ramirez and Sian Beilock asked students to write about their exam worries for 10 minutes, just before a test. They reported the results in 2011.

In the lab, students under pressure who sat quietly scored about 12% lower than on an earlier test. Those who wrote about their worries improved by about 5%. In ninth-grade biology classes, anxious students who wrote about their worries scored nearly one grade point higher on their final exam: say, a B+ instead of a B−. Students low in anxiety did not need it.

Be carefulWorth trying, not guaranteed

In 2018, a large project that repeated 21 high-profile social-science experiments did not find the writing effect again. So treat expressive writing as worth trying, not guaranteed.

ReappraisalYour body getting ready

The second idea is reappraisal. At Harvard, Jeremy Jamieson and colleagues told students preparing for the GRE that feeling keyed up can actually help performance. They scored higher on the maths section of a practice test, and one to three months later, on the real GRE too.

A cautionSmall studies; severe anxiety is different
