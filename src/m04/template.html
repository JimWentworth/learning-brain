The generation effectProducing beats receiving

Sharon Bertsch and colleagues combined 86 studies in 2007. Generating beat reading by 0.40 of a standard deviation, almost half. It held for synonyms, opposites, categories and rhymes, and on both recognition and recall tests.

Generating is a kind of deep processing: to produce the word, you have to search what you know and check that the answer fits.

Self-explanationThe same idea, scaled up

Self-explanation means pausing to explain why a step works, or how one idea connects to another. A 2018 review by Kiran Bisra and colleagues combined 64 research reports with nearly 6,000 learners. Prompting students to explain to themselves improved learning by about half a standard deviation, across many subjects.

Before learningA wrong guess can prepare the mind

Generation even helps before learning. In 2009, Lindsey Richland, Nate Kornell and Liche Kao had students answer questions about an essay before reading it. Most guesses were wrong. Yet those items were learned better than items given the same extra time to study, even counting only the questions students had got wrong.

One cautionClose the loop

In these studies, the correct answer always followed. A wrong guess left uncorrected is just a wrong guess. Generation works when students can make something meaningful and get the right answer soon after, which is also the lesson of the errors and feedback page.

Let them make it first

Before a lecture, pose two or three questions students can’t yet answer, then teach. Leave gaps in notes for students to fill. Pause after a worked step and ask: why does this follow? Have students predict a result before you show it. And always close the loop with the correct answer.
