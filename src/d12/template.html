One thread runs through this whole series: attention and working memory are limited. Good digital design protects them. It removes what distracts, segments what overloads, and asks students to retrieve and think.

The designBefore, during, and after

BeforePlan short video segments, each around one idea. Build slides with a key visual and few words, and cut extras that don’t explain. Offer key readings in a printable form.
DuringPut questions inside videos and lectures. Set device norms, and use devices on purpose. Teach note-taking methods, not just a medium. Use in-person time for what only people in a room can do.
AfterGuide how students use recordings and speed controls: to review, spaced over time, with saved time spent on practice. Check outcomes, not just clicks and views.
