Adults with no juggling experience spent three months learning a three-ball cascade. Brain scans showed more grey matter in an area that tracks visual motion. Three months after they stopped practising, much of that increase had faded.

So adult brains do change, as the neuroplasticity page shows. But notice three things. The change was specific: a motion area, for a motion skill. It took months of effortful practice. And without use, it receded.

PlasticityEarned, specific, kept by use

Some windows also narrow with age, such as learning a new language without an accent. Adult plasticity is real, but it is earned through practice, specific to what is practised, and kept by continued use.

BeliefMindset: true, but not magic

Growth mindset is the idea that ability can be developed, and in one sense it is simply true: skills grow with practice, as the task above shows for most people. But do brief mindset lessons raise grades? In the largest trial, published in 2019 by David Yeager and colleagues, 12,490 ninth graders across the United States took two 25-minute online sessions. Lower-achieving students earned about a tenth of a grade point more in core subjects; higher-achieving students’ grades didn’t change.

Reviews find small average effects. In 2018, Victoria Sisk, Brooke Macnamara and colleagues found the overall effect of mindset interventions on achievement was weak. In a 2023 review, Macnamara and Alexander Burgoyne found that when only the highest-quality studies were kept, the effect was close to zero. Another 2023 review found effects that ranged from negative to positive, depending on the setting.

Some cautionCareful in both directions

Don’t promise that belief alone produces learning. And don’t tell students their abilities are fixed. Both claims are wrong.

Tell the honest version

Skills grow with the right kind of practice, over time. Praise strategy and progress, not effort alone: effort that isn’t working needs a new strategy, not more of the same. Show students their own growth, early work beside later work. And design courses where improvement is possible, with chances to revise and try again.
