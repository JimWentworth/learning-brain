Is younger always better?

Children seem to soak up languages. Adults seem to struggle. A study of two-thirds of a million people put numbers on when language-learning ability changes, and the answer is later than many expected.

Connect it to your course

Many of your students are still learning the language of instruction, and they are fully capable of learning more of it. Judge their understanding by their ideas, and treat grammar errors as part of the process, unless language accuracy is the point of the task.

Good to know

The estimates are about grammar learning and native-like attainment, not about whether adults can become fluent; many do.

Joshua Hartshorne, Joshua Tenenbaum and Steven Pinker put a 10-minute English grammar quiz online. It went viral, and they collected complete data from 669,498 native and non-native speakers, along with each person’s age and the age they started learning English.

Their model separated current age, age at first exposure and years of experience. It estimated that the ability to learn grammar is preserved until about age 17.4 and then declines steadily.

A late windowLater than many thought

Previous debate centred on whether learning ability declines from birth, from around age five, or from puberty. This study placed the end of the critical period for grammar learning much later than previously speculated. The decline held not only for difficult grammar but also for easy constructions that children normally master early.

Why start early?Time to learn

If learning ability stays high until about 17, why does starting early matter? Because becoming native-like takes years of learning. The authors estimated that people need to start before about age 10 to reach native-like grammar, since starting later leaves too little time before the ability declines.

The limitsWhat was measured

The quiz tested English grammar online. It doesn’t speak to vocabulary, pronunciation, fluency or the ability to use a language well for study and work, and “not native-like” is not the same as “not proficient”.

For learningAdults keep learning

Adult students learning in a second language are still building it. Their grammar may stay slightly non-native for life while their command of the subject is excellent. Topic 7 covers what helps adults learn a language most efficiently.
