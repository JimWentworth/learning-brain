Read the transcript

In Richard Mayer’s research, adding on-screen text that repeats the narration word for word to an animation tended to hurt learning on transfer tests. He calls this the redundancy principle.

A related finding is the modality principle. Students learned better from graphics with spoken words than from graphics with printed words. In Mayer’s summary, the median effect was 1.0, positive in 18 of 19 tests.

Captionsare different: optional, and needed by many students

WhyReading and listening compete

Reading and listening to the same words compete for attention. And when the eyes are busy reading, they are not looking at the graphic that the words describe.

An important caveatKeep captions available

These studies tested verbatim text on screen for everyone. Optional captions are different. They support students who are deaf or hard of hearing, and others who need them, and some research suggests they can help non-native speakers. Keep captions available.

For teaching

Put a few key words and a strong visual on each slide, and say the rest. Don’t read your slides aloud. Move detailed text into a handout or notes. And keep captions on for recorded video. Share this tip with students: turn dense slides into your own words.
