Belonging at scale Tell students they belong; then build the course that proves it

A one-hour exercise that halves an achievement gap sounds almost too good to be true. So researchers tested it again, much bigger. It helped, but only where students actually had opportunities to belong.

First, a follow-up of the original students, years later, by Shannon Brady and colleagues, reported better adult outcomes for the Black participants. But that was still one small group, on one campus.

So in 2023, a large team led by Gregory Walton ran the exercise with 26,911 students at 22 colleges and universities. Students did it online, in one short session, before college began.

The resultMore students completed the first year

The exercise raised the share of students who completed their first year as full-time students, especially in groups that had historically progressed at lower rates. The researchers estimated the results could generalize to 749 four-year institutions in the United States. And the online materials are freely available to colleges.

The key findingThe message has to be true

It only worked where students’ groups actually had opportunities to belong. A message about belonging cannot create belonging that isn’t there.

A cautionSmaller effects are normal

The effects were smaller than in the original study. That is normal: large, careful studies usually shrink early results. And small effects, across many students, can still matter.

For teaching

Share the belonging message early, before the first setbacks. Then back it up. Build study groups, learn students’ names, and hold office hours students actually use. Look at who leaves your course, and ask why.
