Connect it to your course

Before class, share key terms and slides. During class, flag technical uses of everyday words, speak at a steady pace, point and gesture, and prompt explanation. After class, caption recordings, use short writing to check understanding, and bring key terms back in quizzes.

The topics in this series point the same way. Language is a specialised system, distinct from reasoning. Words build up over many encounters and stick through recall. Reading and listening depend on knowing nearly all the words. Explaining, writing and gesturing turn ideas into language, and help learning.

None of these moves is only for second-language learners. They make a course clearer for everyone, and essential for some.

BeforePrepare the words

Students can follow speech when they know 95–98% of the words. Post key terms with definitions and example sentences before class, and share slides or notes so students can preview them. Flag everyday words that have technical meanings in your field.

DuringMake meaning visible

Speak at a steady pace, pause after key points, and face the room. Point to what you are describing and use your hands to show shapes and changes. Ask students to explain steps to themselves and to each other.

AfterBring the language back

Caption recordings. Use a minute of writing to see what students understood. Quiz key terms over several weeks, asking students to recall them, not just recognise them. When grading, decide whether you are assessing the ideas, the language, or both.

A cautionPrinciples, not a formula

Much of the evidence comes from language classrooms, school settings or lab studies with short materials. The effects vary with learners and contexts. Use these moves as defaults and ask your students what helps them.
