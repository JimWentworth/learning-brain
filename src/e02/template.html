The resultHalf a standard deviation

Class quizzes raised achievement by about half a standard deviation, an effect size of 0.5. That is large by the standards of education research. And it held across school levels and subjects.

The benefit was not limited to facts. Quizzing also helped with understanding concepts and solving problems, which counters the worry that testing is just drill.

In practiceDetails that help

Format mattered less than expected: multiple choice and short answer both worked. Graded and ungraded quizzes both helped. Repeated quizzing across days helped more than a single quiz. Whether students answered alone or in groups made little difference.

WhyThree reasons

The authors point to extra exposure to the material, practice in the form the final test will take, and motivation to keep up. Quizzes also show instructors where the class is, in time to adjust.

For teaching

Give short, frequent quizzes, weekly or even every class. Keep them low stakes. Repeat key questions across weeks. And let quizzes look like the questions students will face on exams. Share this tip with students: treat quizzes as practice, not judgment.
