Crutch or tutor Producing the answer is what teaches

With AI in hand, students did far better in practice. Take it away, and those who had used a standard chatbot did worse than students who never had it. A tutor that gave hints did not.

Nearly a thousand high-school students in Turkey practised maths with one of two AI tutors, or with no AI at all. One tutor was a standard chat interface, like ChatGPT. The other was prompted to give hints, not answers. Hamsa Bastani and colleagues reported the results in 2025.

During practice, both tutors helped a lot. Then the AI was taken away for the exam.

~1,000high-school maths students in a field experiment
+48%in practice with a standard chat, and +127% with the hint-giving tutor
−17%on the exam without AI, for students who had practised with the standard chat
About the sameas students who never had AI, for those who had the hint-giving tutor

WhyA crutch skips the effort that builds skill

The logs suggested why. Without guardrails, many students used the AI as a crutch: they asked for the answer and copied it. The effort that builds skill never happened.

This is the testing and generation effects from Series 2, run in reverse. Retrieving and generating answers build lasting memories because they take effort. Getting the answer is easy. Producing it is what teaches.

A cautionOne school, one subject

This was one school and one subject, covering about 15% of the curriculum, with a model from 2023, and the students were in high school, not university. And the hint-giving tutor prevented the harm, but it did not raise exam scores.

Make students do the climbing

If students use AI in practice, give them a tutor that hints rather than solves. Grade the process, not just the answer. And always include some practice without AI, so you and they can see what they can do alone.
